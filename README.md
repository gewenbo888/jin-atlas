# Jin Language Atlas · 晋语图谱

A bilingual (English / 中文) academic atlas of Jin Chinese (晋语) — the major Sinitic variety of the Loess Plateau, spoken across Shanxi, northern Shaanxi, central Inner Mongolia, and western Hebei.

The Atlas locates Jin in geography, sound change, and the political history of the Three Jins, especially the State of Zhao. It is structured around eight modules.

## Eight modules

1. **Overview** — definition (Li Rong 1985 classification), distribution, eight sub-groups, the diagnostic feature (entering tone retention).
2. **Linguistic Feature Engine** — 5-tone system, finals (Middle Chinese reflexes), consonants, distinctive morphology (圪 prefix, 哩 particle), all compared with Mandarin.
3. **Dialect Map** — interactive SVG map with Lüliang, Taihang, Yellow River geography overlaid; click any of the 8 sub-groups for cities, traits, speaker counts.
4. **Historical Evolution Timeline** — Old Chinese (1200 BCE) → Middle Chinese → Mandarin's loss of the entering tone (~1100–1400 CE) → Li Rong's modern classification.
5. **Zhao State Connection** *(critical)* — fact panel, schematic territorial overlay, five reasoning blocks: territory, population continuity, linguistic implication, the explicit caveat against state-equals-language fallacy, and where this leaves the Atlas's claim.
6. **Geography × Language** — Taihang, Lüliang, Yellow River, Loess Plateau, Fen Valley as load-bearing variables.
7. **Cultural System** — Jin Opera, Pu Opera, Shangdang Bangzi, the 圪/猴/骚情 idiom register, noodle and aged-vinegar food culture.
8. **Comparison Engine** — Jin vs Mandarin vs other Sinitic groups (Wu, Cantonese, Min, Hakka, Xiang, Gan) on phonology, vocabulary, and grammar.

## Method note

The Atlas follows mainstream Sinitic-linguistics conventions: Li Rong (1985) for the classification; Pulleyblank and Baxter–Sagart-style references for Middle Chinese reconstructions; speaker-count estimates from Ethnologue / 中国语言地图集. Reconstructions are explicitly marked as such; the Atlas avoids origin-myth narratives and tourist linguistics.
